What Is a Cool Flame

  • A ~650 K flame with weak reaction: it releases little heat, light, and CO₂.
  • Davy observed it in 1817 (hot platinum wire in diethyl ether vapor); Emeléus recorded its emission and coined the term in 1929.
  • Until 2012–2014 it was seen as a premixed-mixture phenomenon leading to ignition, typical of engine knock.

The Discovery on the ISS (FLEX, 2011)

  • In 2011, in the FLEX experiment on the International Space Station, a large n-heptane droplet showed "anomalous" combustion that was later identified as a cool flame on a droplet.
  • This revived research on multiphase cool flames (2012–2014).

Why the Cool Flame Appears

  • Radiative heat loss pushes the droplet from the first stage (hot flame) to the second (cool flame).
  • A dynamic balance between heat generation and heat loss sustains the long-lasting cool-flame burning.

Multi-Stage Oscillatory Flames and Pressure

  • n-heptane model (4.08 mm, 3 atm, O₂/CO₂/N₂ = 0.21/0.15/0.64) with cycles of high-temperature ignition, radiative extinction, low-temperature reaction with self-heating, and reignition.
  • The n-heptane ignition delay depends on pressure and on the degenerate branching in the kinetics (slide 17).

Three Stages with Helium Substitution (3 atm)

  • More helium increases the burning rate; at high helium a quasi-steady third stage appears: hot (~950 K), moderate, and cool (~770 K).
  • With more helium the second stage is suppressed; the droplet diameter barely changes because the flame contracts and compensates.
  • Radiative and diffusive losses are comparable in the first two stages; in the third, diffusive losses dominate.
  • Confirmed with n-dodecane at 2.75 atm (21.6% O₂, 50.2% N₂, 28.2% He): qualitative agreement with the model.

Small Droplets and Ozone

  • Question: can a cool flame be triggered in small droplets? Ozone helps, and a minimum concentration is needed to sustain combustion.
  • Near-limit case (0.5 mm droplet, 1 atm, 298 K, 10% O₂, 5% O₃): the flame oscillates; the steady phase lasts only ~0.12 s at ~710 K.

Ground Experiments: Counterflow Diffusion Flame

  • It is an ideal configuration for studying flame strength under stretch and for validating kinetics and transport.
  • The usual numerical approach (the one-dimensional OPPDIF module) assumes the results depend only on the axial coordinate and does not represent low-strain cool flames well.
  • Buoyancy lifts the flame; a 2D model captures the flow perturbation and the effect of the Richardson number, and at higher strain the flame moves away from the nozzle.
