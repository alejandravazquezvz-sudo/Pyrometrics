Extinction Characteristics of n-Alkane Droplets

Farouk and Dryer · Combustion Symposium 2022 (Vancouver) · 18 slides

Burning Modes of Three Fuels

  • n-heptane, n-decane, and n-dodecane: two-stage burning (hot and cool); n-dodecane at elevated pressure reaches three stages.
  • n-decane: the ignition energy decides whether the hot flame forms, and radiative loss triggers the transition to a cool flame. With low ignition energy there is single-stage cool-flame burning, which depends on the flash point.

Model and Validation

  • Droplet model with C7–C20 kinetics (583 species, 2609 reactions) and radiation.
  • Measured and predicted extinction diameters are compared at 1 atm in air (slide 9). In the qualitative comparison for n-dodecane at 2.75 atm, the model parameters were not tuned (slide 6).

See the original: p. 6 · p. 7 · p. 9

Behavior at 1 atm in Air

  • At the transition to the second stage, the temperature and the stagnation ratio are nearly the same for the three fuels.
  • A longer carbon chain gives a hotter cool flame (≈775 K for C12, 737 K for C10, 720 K for C7) and a smaller cool-flame extinction diameter.
  • Hot-flame extinction barely changes between fuels (it is governed by small-species kinetics); cool-flame extinction does, and it is dictated by the decomposition of •O₂QOOH.

See the original: p. 10 · p. 11

Effect of Pressure

  • Up to 1 atm there are two stages; at 1.25 atm, repeated hot–cool–hot–cool cycles; at 3 atm, a stable "warm" flame.
  • The cool-flame extinction diameter decreases with pressure and disappears at 3 atm, where the droplet burns completely.

See the original: p. 12 · p. 15

Conclusions

  • The transition to the second stage occurs universally near ~1200 K.
  • Longer n-alkanes give more robust cool flames and smaller extinction diameters.
  • The cool-flame extinction diameter varies linearly with the initial diameter, but it does not collapse onto a single curve as the hot-flame one does.
