Three-Stage Burning: Hot, Warm, and Cool

Farouk, Dietrich, and Dryer · Combustion Symposium 2018 (Dublin) · 22 slides

Cool Flames Triggered by Radiative Losses

  • First observed in n-heptane droplets on the ISS (2011); with CO₂ as diluent, radiative losses increase and multi-stage burning appears.

See the original: p. 2 · p. 3

The Question: More Diffusive Losses

  • Slide 5 poses the question of whether conditions that increase diffusive losses and reactivity (helium as diluent and higher pressure) affect burning.
  • A transient droplet model is used, with detailed high- and low-temperature kinetics and radiation.

See the original: p. 5 · p. 6

Three Stages with Helium at 3 and 5 atm

  • More helium raises the burning rate and a quasi-steady third stage appears.
  • At high helium the second stage is suppressed. At 5 atm the same behavior is seen, but with more helium.
  • The flame contraction keeps the droplet diameter nearly the same despite the temperature drop.
  • Heat generation and losses are strongly coupled; diffusive losses dominate the third stage.

Kinetics and Species

  • Low-temperature (QOOH) chemistry is what predominates in the third stage.
  • There is competition between adding O₂ to QOOH and decomposing QOOH (OH, HO₂, olefins, carbonyls).

See the original: p. 14

Experimental Confirmation in FLEX

  • FLEX 1268: n-dodecane, 2.75 atm, 21.6% O₂, 50.2% N₂, 28.2% He, with qualitative agreement with the predictions.
  • FLEX 1256: n-dodecane, 3.00 atm, air, ~4.2 mm droplet.

See the original: p. 15 · p. 21

Conclusions

  • There are two quasi-steady cool-flame modes in droplets: "warm" (NTC regime) and "cool" (low temperature).
  • Helium is critical: it either promotes or completely suppresses the second stage.
  • The n-dodecane experiments validate the a priori prediction made with n-heptane.
