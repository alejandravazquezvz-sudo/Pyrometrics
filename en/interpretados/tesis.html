Doctoral Dissertation of M. F. Alam (USC, 2018)

"Combustion behavior of sub-millimeter sized oxygenated and n-alkane droplets" · 227 pages · chapters 6 and 7 are the ones relevant to PSI-117

Dissertation Summary

  • Using CFD and detailed kinetics, it studies three topics: fire suppression in microgravity, refinement of butanol kinetic models, and cool flames initiated directly in small droplets.

Ch. 3 — Xenon as a Fire Suppressant (background)

  • With methanol as the model fuel, xenon lengthens the burning time and raises the flame temperature; it sustains a hot flame with less oxygen.
  • Its low thermal diffusivity makes it a worse suppressant; NASA postponed testing it.

See the original: p. 59 · p. 60

Chs. 4 and 5 — Butanol and Its Isomers (background)

  • The model of Sarathy et al. predicted burning that was too fast; a perturbation analysis pointed to the transport parameters of each isomer.
  • After updating them, the predictions improved significantly.

Ch. 6 — Ozone-Assisted Cool Flame

  • Ozone releases atomic oxygen, produces extra heat, and stabilizes the cool flame near the droplet.
  • The flash point decides: n-decane tends toward damped oscillation and requires more ozone than n-heptane.

See the original: p. 154 · p. 155

Ch. 7 — Oscillatory Cool Flame Near the Limit

  • Near the limit the flame does not reach steady burning and oscillates ever more strongly; this is caused by QOOH chemistry and fuel leakage.

See the original: p. 193

Ch. 8 — Conclusion

  • Droplet simulations are useful for predicting extinction with untested suppressants, validating and refining kinetics, and understanding cool flames in sub-millimeter droplets.
