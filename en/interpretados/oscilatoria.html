Oscillatory Cool Flame in Sub-Millimeter Droplets

F. Alam, Dryer, and Farouk · ASGSR 2018 Annual Meeting · 19 slides

Motivation

  • Relevant to engine knock and autoignition, the lean flammability limit, spacecraft fire safety, and low-temperature kinetics.

See the original: p. 2

Simulated Condition

  • 0.5 mm n-heptane droplet, 1 atm, 298 K, 10% O₂ and 5% O₃ with N₂ balance.
  • 1D droplet model with detailed kinetics and narrow-band radiation.

See the original: p. 4 · p. 6

Observed Behavior

  • The ignition energy raises the local temperature above ~1150 K; the flame then expands and cools.
  • It contracts in less than 0.1 s and the steady cool flame lasts only ~0.12 s (~710 K), after which it enters continuous oscillation.

See the original: p. 7

How the Oscillation Starts

  • Ozone locally raises the O₂ fraction (0.10 → 0.16), and this difference drives O₂ to diffuse outward, leaving a local void.
  • The pulsations begin when the local O₂ falls below ~13.5%; the cycle repeats.

See the original: p. 8

Role of Low-Temperature Chemistry

  • The oscillation arises from the interplay between degenerate branching and QOOH propagation/termination, in the negative temperature coefficient (NTC) region.

See the original: p. 9

Fuel Leakage and Conclusion

  • Continuous fuel leakage through the flame makes the oscillation grow outward.
  • With ozone the droplet attempts a steady cool flame but fails to achieve it and enters an ever-growing oscillation.
